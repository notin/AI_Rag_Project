# Pokémon Type Matchups and Battle Strategy

The type system is the foundation of all Pokémon battling. Each Pokémon has one or two types that determine which attacks it resists, which attacks deal extra damage to it, and which attacks it is completely immune to. Understanding these relationships is essential for both casual play and competitive strategy.

## The 15 Original Types

Generation I introduced 15 of the 18 types that exist today. The original types are: Normal, Fire, Water, Grass, Electric, Ice, Fighting, Poison, Ground, Flying, Psychic, Bug, Rock, Ghost, and Dragon. The Dark, Steel, and Fairy types were added in later generations to address balance issues in the original type chart.

## Key Type Advantages

**Fire** beats Grass, Bug, and Ice. Fire-type attacks simulate burning, which naturally overcomes plant matter, insect exoskeletons, and frozen structures. However, Fire is weak to Water, Rock, and Ground — representing how fire is extinguished by water, smothered by earth, or starved of fuel.

**Water** beats Fire, Rock, and Ground. Water erodes rock, extinguishes flame, and dissolves earth. Water is weak to Grass and Electric — plants absorb water for growth, and water conducts electricity dangerously.

**Grass** beats Water, Rock, and Ground. Plants drink water, crack through rock with their roots, and anchor soil. Grass is weak to Fire, Ice, Poison, Bug, and Flying — making it the type with the most weaknesses, which is a significant competitive disadvantage.

**Electric** beats Water and Flying. Electricity flows through water and strikes airborne targets. Electric is only weak to Ground, as the earth absorbs electrical current. Notably, Electric-type Pokémon cannot be paralyzed by Electric moves.

**Psychic** was the most overpowered type in Generation I, with only one true weakness (Bug) and no viable counters. Ghost-type moves should have been super effective against Psychic, but a programming bug made Psychic immune to Ghost instead. This, combined with the Psychic type's dominance over Fighting and Poison (two of the most common types), made Psychic Pokémon virtually unstoppable in Generation I.

## Important Immunities

Certain type matchups result in complete immunity — zero damage regardless of attack power:

- **Normal and Fighting** cannot hit Ghost types (ghosts are intangible to physical strikes)
- **Ground** cannot hit Flying types (airborne Pokémon avoid earthquakes)
- **Electric** cannot hit Ground types (the earth absorbs all electrical current)
- **Poison** cannot hit Steel types (added in Gen II — metal cannot be poisoned)
- **Psychic** cannot hit Dark types (added in Gen II — the dark mind resists psychic probing)
- **Ghost** cannot hit Normal types (the mundane is unaffected by the supernatural)
- **Dragon** cannot hit Fairy types (added in Gen VI — fairy magic nullifies draconic power)

## STAB (Same-Type Attack Bonus)

When a Pokémon uses a move that matches its own type, the move's power is multiplied by 1.5. This bonus, called STAB (Same-Type Attack Bonus), is one of the most important damage calculations in the game. For example, a Fire-type Pokémon using Flamethrower gets 50% more damage compared to a Normal-type Pokémon using the same move.

This mechanic means that a Pokémon's offensive potential is heavily tied to its typing. Dual-typed Pokémon benefit from STAB on two types of moves, giving them broader coverage. A Water/Ice type like Lapras gets STAB on both Surf and Ice Beam, allowing it to hit a wide range of targets with boosted power.

## Competitive Strategy Basics

Successful competitive teams consider type coverage, meaning the ability to hit as many opposing types for super-effective damage as possible. The ideal moveset covers the team's weaknesses while maintaining STAB on at least one move.

Common defensive cores pair Pokémon whose type resistances complement each other. For example, a Steel-type and a Water-type together resist or are immune to 15 of the 18 types between them. This concept, called "type synergy," forms the backbone of team building at every level of competitive play.

Weather conditions (Rain, Sun, Sand, Hail) can dramatically shift type advantages by boosting certain types while weakening others. Rain boosts Water moves by 50% while halving Fire moves, effectively reversing one of the core type matchups. Teams built around weather are called "weather teams" and represent one of the most popular competitive archetypes.
