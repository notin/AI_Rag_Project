# Ghost-Type Pokémon of Kanto

The Ghost-type Pokémon of Kanto are among the most mysterious and feared creatures in the region. In Generation I, only a single evolutionary line of Ghost types existed — Gastly, Haunter, and Gengar — making them the sole representatives of this otherworldly typing. Their connection to Lavender Town and the Pokémon Tower gives them a uniquely eerie place in Pokémon lore.

## Lavender Town and the Pokémon Tower

Lavender Town is a small, somber settlement in eastern Kanto known primarily as a resting place for deceased Pokémon. At the heart of the town stands Pokémon Tower, a seven-story memorial building where trainers come to mourn and pay respects to their departed partners. The tower is haunted by the spirits of Pokémon, and without the Silph Scope — a device that reveals the true form of ghost apparitions — visitors cannot identify or battle the spectral entities that inhabit its upper floors.

The most famous ghost in the tower is the spirit of a Marowak, a mother who was killed by Team Rocket while protecting her child Cubone. This restless spirit blocks passage through the tower until the player uses the Silph Scope to reveal its identity and puts it to rest. The emotional weight of this encounter is one of the most memorable moments in the original games.

## Gastly — The Gas Pokémon

Gastly is a Ghost/Poison dual-type Pokémon composed almost entirely of gaseous matter. It has no solid body — its form is a sphere of toxic gas surrounded by a thin, dark membrane. Gastly can envelop an opponent in its gaseous body to poison and suffocate them. In windy conditions, Gastly's body can be blown apart, so it tends to inhabit enclosed spaces like old buildings, caves, and abandoned structures.

Despite its fragile appearance, Gastly is notable for its complete immunity to Normal and Fighting-type attacks due to its Ghost typing, and its Poison typing gives it resistance to Bug, Grass, and Fairy moves. Its Levitate ability provides an additional immunity to Ground-type attacks, giving it an impressive three complete immunities.

## Haunter — The Gas Pokémon

Haunter evolves from Gastly at level 25 and gains a more defined, sinister form. It lurks in dark corners and licks its victims with a spectral tongue that causes uncontrollable shivering and eventual fainting. Haunter is said to inhabit dark, abandoned places and prey on anyone who enters its territory by beckoning them with a disembodied, floating hand.

In the anime, a Haunter befriended Ash Ketchum and helped him battle Sabrina, the Psychic-type Gym Leader of Saffron City. Haunter's comedic antics made Sabrina laugh for the first time, breaking the psychic trance that had trapped her personality. This Haunter chose to stay with Sabrina rather than join Ash's team.

## Gengar — The Shadow Pokémon

Gengar is the fully evolved form, obtained when Haunter is traded to another trainer. This trade evolution mechanic means that Gengar requires social interaction to obtain, fitting its nature as a trickster that thrives on the company of others — even if that company involves terrifying them.

Gengar hides in the shadows of rooms, and its presence causes the temperature to drop by nearly 10 degrees Fahrenheit. It places curses on people by hiding in their shadows, and the malicious grin perpetually plastered on its face suggests it derives genuine enjoyment from scaring others. Despite this, Gengar is theorized to have once been human — some Pokédex entries suggest it may be a departed human spirit trying to create a companion by cursing the living.

In competitive battling, Gengar is an elite threat with excellent Special Attack (130) and Speed (110). Its movepool is vast: Shadow Ball, Sludge Bomb, Focus Blast, Thunderbolt, Energy Ball, and the support move Will-O-Wisp. Mega Gengar's Shadow Tag ability prevents opponents from switching out, creating devastating trap scenarios. Gengar has been a staple in the OU (Overused) competitive tier since the inception of competitive Pokémon.
